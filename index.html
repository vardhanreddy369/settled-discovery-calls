15 minutes on how your events actually run

We are five engineering students at UCF, researching how people who run events keep budgets and payments on track. Nothing to sell, we only want to learn how it really works.

No demo and no sales follow up. This is research.

Why a student is emailing you

EGN 4641C is UCF's engineering entrepreneurship course, taught by Dr. Heinrich. It is engineering students being taught to build a business rather than a product, and the first half of the semester is nothing but customer interviews.

We are not building or pitching anything this semester. Our job is to find out whether the problem is real, which is why you are getting a request for your time instead of a sales email.

What we are trying to learn

Our question is where the money side of an event breaks down. Budgets, cost tracking and reporting usually sit in separate spreadsheets and documents, and nothing checks that they still agree as the event changes.

Most of what gets written about running events describes how it is supposed to go. We want the version with the messy parts left in. There are no right answers here, and in our experience the unglamorous answers are the useful ones.

The six questions

This is the whole list, so you can see it before you agree to anything. We are hoping you will walk us through real examples rather than answer in the abstract. Happy to skip anything that does not apply.

  1. 01Walk us through the last event you ran, from the first budget to the final numbers.
  2. 02Where do those numbers first live, and who else needs to see them?
  3. 03How do you track costs and approvals once the event is underway?
  4. 04What happens when a cost changes after the budget is set?
  5. 05With several events running at once, how do you keep them straight?
  6. 06Has a final number ever not matched what was expected? What happened?
